Terms & Privacy

The page that exists so nobody can say they weren't warned. Here's what actually happens to your data, written so you might actually finish reading it.

1. What even is this

OrbitOS is a hobby project built by one person to see how far a browser tab could be stretched into looking like an operating system. It is not Windows. It is not Linux. It is not a company, and there is no support team standing by. There's just me and however much free time I've got that week.

It's a sandbox, so yes, it lags, bugs out, and occasionally faceplants for no obvious reason. I fix what I can when I can. Using OrbitOS means you understand it's a fun experiment and not something backed by an uptime guarantee. Enterprise-grade stability was never on the table.

2. The only data I actually collect

Let's get this over with: I don't care what you're doing in here enough to spy on it. The only tracking on this site is basic Google Analytics.

It tells me things like how many people showed up today and whether mobile users are having a worse time than desktop ones. That's it. It does not tell me what you typed in the terminal, what apps you installed, or anything about your files. I couldn't see that even if I felt like it, which I don't.

3. Local storage, or where your stuff actually lives

There is no server somewhere quietly hoarding your files. Everything you do inside OrbitOS is saved locally, in your own browser, through a database wrapper called OrbitDB. None of it touches a server, which also means I physically cannot read your notes even out of curiosity. Here's what actually gets stored, and why it needs to be.

A. Identity & Security

Setting up the OS saves your username and hostname so the terminal has something to greet you with. Set a lock screen password and the OS does not save the actual password, because that would be a genuinely bad idea. It runs it through SHA-256 and stores the scrambled result instead. Forget your password and you're locked out, because the system truly does not know what it was.

B. Your personal files (Notes, Gallery, Tasks)

This is the actual point of the OS. Notes, to-do items, and calendar entries all get saved as plain text in your browser's database. Import a photo into Gallery and the OS converts it into a wall of Base64 text and stores that, so it can load the image again later without needing a cloud drive.

C. AI chat memory

AI assistants have the memory of a goldfish by default. To fake an actual conversation, OrbitOS keeps your recent messages (orbitos_ai_memory) locally and sends them along with every new prompt so the AI has some idea what you two were just talking about.

D. Browser & media logs

The internal Orbit Browser keeps a history and bookmarks log, same as any browser, just scoped to this one tab's database. The Music Player saves the songs you favorite or played recently, so your playlist survives a reboot instead of resetting every time.

E. Aesthetics (themes & customization)

Every theme, color, blur setting, and wallpaper you pick gets saved. Rebuilding your setup from scratch every time you opened a tab would be a special kind of misery, for both of us.

F. API keys & AI persona

Plug in your own Gemini API key and it stays in your browser. I never see it. It travels straight from your browser to Google when you send a prompt, and nowhere near me. Any custom persona you wrote for the AI gets saved the same local way.

G. System junk

The OS tracks which apps you installed through APT so it knows what commands to allow, plus some background error logs, so when something crashes you (or future me) can actually figure out what broke.

H. Temporary session data

sessionStorage tracks Safe Mode status and counts failed boots in a row. Crash three times back to back and it drops you into the Recovery Menu automatically. This clears the second you close the tab.

4. External APIs, or who OrbitOS actually talks to

To function like an OS, it has to talk to the real internet. There's no backend sitting in the middle, your browser calls these APIs directly. Here's exactly what leaves your device and where it goes.

  • Google Gemini (AI assistant): Typing a prompt sends your text, your recent chat history, and your API key straight to Google's generativelanguage.googleapis.com. If you gave the AI permission to run commands, it also gets basic info about your OS state so it knows what's actually possible.
  • Weather & geocoding: Getting the weather means pinging GeoJS first, which checks your IP to guess your city, then handing that city name to Open-Meteo for the actual temperature, forecast, and air quality.
  • Wikipedia app: Searching the Wiki app sends your exact query to Wikipedia's own API for the article summary, HTML content, and thumbnail.
  • Music (Audius API): Searching for a song sends your query to api.audius.co, which also streams the audio straight to your player.
  • Assorted utilities: Depending what you run, text might go to MyMemory for translation, ExchangeRate-API for currency conversion, IPify to check your public IP, qrserver.com to generate a QR code, or through a CORS proxy (allorigins.win) to pull RSS feeds your browser would otherwise refuse to load.

5. Backups & sharing (read this one)

Since everything lives in your browser, OrbitOS has a built in Backup & Restore tool. You can export your whole system as a raw .json file straight to your actual hard drive. Treat that file like it's radioactive.

Fair warning: a backup file contains everything you've saved. Notes, tasks, browsing history, API keys, all of it. Send that file to a friend and they can restore it and see literally everything. If you hand someone your backup and regret it later, that's a you problem, not an OrbitOS problem.

6. Data loss & system wipes (also important)

Since everything lives in your browser's local storage, none of it is actually safe long term.

Clear your browsing data and OrbitOS goes with it. Run low on phone storage and your browser might quietly delete it to make room without asking you first. And if I ship a large enough architecture change, sometimes I have to wipe everyone's storage just to get the new system working at all.

Short version: your data can vanish at any point without warning, and using OrbitOS means you've accepted that. Don't store anything you'd actually miss. No passwords, no seed phrases, no unsaved school essays. If it disappears, that's between you and your browser.

7. Just use it normally

This is a free hobby project on a regular domain, so just don't be a menace. Don't try to inject something malicious, don't try to break it for everyone else, and please don't DDoS it. It's hosted on Render, so there's no dramatic server room to bring down, and I genuinely cannot imagine what would motivate someone to DDoS a random hobby OS in the first place. Also worth noting, most of this runs client side in your own browser, so attacking it mostly means attacking yourself. Bold strategy.

8. Updates to this page

I'll update this page when something big enough changes under the hood to matter. If it's a big deal, I'll post about it on the main site or in the OrbitOS News app so it's not a total surprise.

That's the boring part done. Go boot the thing up.
